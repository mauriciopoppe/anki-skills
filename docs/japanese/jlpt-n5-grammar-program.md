# JLPT N5 Grammar Program

This is a light, structured grammar track to run alongside sentence mining,
comprehensible input, and daily Anki reviews. It assumes a vocabulary of roughly
400 words. The goal is not to stop learning from real Japanese; it is to make
common grammar easier to notice and understand when it appears.

## How to use the program

Study one unit at a time. A unit is complete when you can:

- recognize the pattern in a new sentence;
- explain its role in plain English;
- form one affirmative and, when applicable, one negative example; and
- understand it in audio at natural but clear speed.

For each unit:

1. Read the short explanation and study the formation rule.
2. Review the examples aloud and shadow their audio when available.
3. Find two examples in mined content or Japanese media.
4. Add no more than two focused grammar cards to Anki.
5. Write or say two sentences about daily life using known vocabulary.
6. Revisit the unit after one week. Move on when the completion criteria above
   feel comfortable; perfection is not required.

Keep grammar cards **i+1**: the grammar pattern should be the only new element.
Prefer familiar, high-frequency vocabulary and reuse a sentence for multiple
learning targets when doing so reduces cognitive load.

## Unit 1.1 — Polite requests

### Goal

Ask someone politely to do something or not to do something, and recognize both
patterns in speech and writing.

### Pattern A: 〜てください

Use a verb in its **て-form** followed by **ください** to make a polite request:

> **て-form + ください** — “Please do …”

Examples:

- 名前を**教えてください**。— Please tell me your name.
- ここに**書いてください**。— Please write here.
- ちょっと**待ってください**。— Please wait a moment.

Although **ください** is polite, it is still a direct request. With strangers or
in delicate situations, a question such as 〜てもらえますか may sound softer;
that distinction can wait until later.

### Pattern B: 〜ないでください

Use the verb's plain negative **ない-form**, add **で**, and then **ください**:

> **ない-form + でください** — “Please do not …”

Examples:

- 部屋に**入らないでください**。— Please do not enter the room.
- ここで写真を**撮らないでください**。— Please do not take pictures here.
- それを**食べないでください**。— Please do not eat that.

Do not confuse this with **〜てはいけません**, which states that something is
not allowed. **〜ないでください** is a request directed at a person.

### Conjugation refresher

#### て-form

| Dictionary ending | Change | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| う, つ, る | って | 待つ → 待って |
| む, ぶ, ぬ | んで | 読む → 読んで |
| く | いて | 書く → 書いて |
| ぐ | いで | 泳ぐ → 泳いで |
| す | して | 話す → 話して |
| Ichidan 〜る | remove る + て | 食べる → 食べて |
| Irregular | — | する → して; 来る → 来て |

Exception: 行く becomes **行って**.

#### ない-form

- Godan verb: change the final **u-row** kana to its **a-row** equivalent and
  add ない. For verbs ending in う, use わ: 書く → 書かない; 買う → 買わない.
- Ichidan verb: remove る and add ない: 食べる → 食べない.
- Irregular: する → しない; 来る → 来ない; ある → ない.

### Practice

Convert each cue into a polite request, then say it aloud before checking the
answer.

1. 読む — ask someone to read it.
2. 座る — ask someone to sit here.
3. 忘れる — ask someone not to forget.
4. 入る — ask someone not to enter.
5. 写真を撮る — ask someone to take a picture.
6. 日本語を話す — ask someone not to speak Japanese.

Answers:

1. 読んでください。
2. ここに座ってください。
3. 忘れないでください。
4. 入らないでください。
5. 写真を撮ってください。
6. 日本語を話さないでください。

### Production task

Create four short requests from your daily life:

- two with **〜てください**;
- two with **〜ないでください**;
- at least one Godan verb and one Ichidan verb; and
- only vocabulary you already know, apart from the grammar pattern.

### Mining task

During immersion, collect two naturally occurring examples—one positive and one
negative request. Keep the original audio and surrounding sentence when
possible. Compare the speaker, listener, and situation: directness is easier to
remember when it is tied to context.

### Suggested Anki card

Front:

> ここに名前を（書く → polite request）。

Back:

> ここに名前を**書いてください**。
> **て-form + ください** = a polite request to do something.

Create a second card for **〜ないでください** only if the negative formation is
not already easy to recall.

## Program notes

The original outline described every item as material needed for the JLPT N5.
In practice, JLPT does not publish an official exhaustive grammar list, and
third-party resources classify some patterns differently. Treat the phases as a
useful learning order rather than an official syllabus. The track also needs a
future foundations section covering sentence structure, adjective and verb
conjugation, counters, and the core particles; those should be assessed before
assuming the numbered units alone cover N5.

AI-generated explanations and examples can contain mistakes. Verify anything
that seems unusual against a trusted grammar reference or a proficient speaker.
